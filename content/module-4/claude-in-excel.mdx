Claude doesn't only work in a chat window. It can also work inside Microsoft Excel, right next to your spreadsheet.

## Advanced Data Operations

Use Claude directly inside Excel to analyse, explain, and act on your spreadsheet data — no switching tabs or copy-pasting.

- **Formula Assistance:** Write, fix, and explain complex <Term def="a calculation in a spreadsheet cell, such as adding up a column.">formulas</Term> in plain English
- **Data Analysis:** Summarise trends, flag <Term def="numbers that don't fit the pattern of the rest.">anomalies</Term>, and interpret results across large datasets
- **Audit & Validation:** Trace logic errors and verify <Term def="the rules a business's numbers are meant to follow.">business rules</Term> step-by-step

## Try it: what does the weather data show?

This part needs access; see [how hands-on access works](/access). Open Claude using the access your Code for All session gives you.

### Open Claude in Excel

Claude for Excel is an <Term def="a small extra you add to a program to give it a new ability.">add-in</Term>. It works in Excel on the web, in Excel on Windows with Microsoft 365, and in Excel on a Mac (version 16.46 or later). It doesn't work on an iPad or on Android, so use a laptop or a Chromebook for this part.

Don't have Excel? Excel on the web is free with a Microsoft account. Signing in to Claude uses the access your Code for All session gives you, so there's nothing to buy.

1. Open the **Claude for Microsoft 365** listing on Microsoft AppSource and choose **Get it now**.
2. Open Excel.
3. Open the add-in. In Excel on Windows and on the web: **Home**, then **Add-ins**. On a Mac: **Tools**, then **Add-ins**.
4. Sign in with your Claude account, using the access your Code for All session gives you.

These steps come from the official [Claude for Excel docs](https://claude.com/docs/office-agents/excel). Menus move around, so if a button isn't where this says, look for **Add-ins**.

### Get the data

Download the data: <a href="/lessons/module-4/singapore-weather.csv" download>singapore-weather.csv</a>

It holds 90 days of daily weather for Singapore, from 28 June to 25 September 2026: the highest, lowest and average temperature, the rainfall, the hours of rain, the highest wind speed and the hours of sunshine for each day.

Weather data: [Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/) (CC BY 4.0)

<Callout kind="tryit">
Find out what the data shows, and explain it in your own words.
</Callout>

1. Open singapore-weather.csv in Excel.
2. Open Claude and ask it to explain what the data shows. For example, ask about trends, the hottest and wettest days, and whether rainy days are cooler.
3. Check its answer against the cells. If Claude says a day was the wettest, find that date and look at its Rainfall (mm) cell.
4. Explain what the data shows in your own words, in two or three sentences.

Here's one way to ask. It's only an example, so change it however you like:

```prompt title="Example question"
Explain what this weather data shows. What are the main trends? Which were the hottest and wettest days? Are rainy days cooler than dry days? Tell me which cells you used for each answer.
```
