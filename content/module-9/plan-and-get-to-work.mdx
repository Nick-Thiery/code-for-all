You've picked a project and checked it against the requirements. Now plan it, so you build the right thing first.

<PlanBoardDiagram />

## 1. Clarify your ideas on a board

The class used a Miro board: an online whiteboard where you put ideas on sticky notes and move them around. Any board works, even paper. Get every idea out, then group them.

## 2. Set up a shared folder

The class used a shared Google Drive with a master doc and task sheets.

- The **master doc** says what the project is, who it's for and what "done" looks like. That's your project brief.
- The **task sheets** list what needs doing, who's doing it and whether it's finished.

Working alone? One document with those two parts is enough. Your own notes work too.

## 3. Write your project brief

The brief is one page: the problem, who has it, what your project does about it, and the hardest part. Save it here so [Module 10](/module-10) can show it back to you.

<Callout kind="headsup">
Keep personal details out of your plan and your project: no full names, school, address or phone numbers, yours or anyone else's. If your project collects anything from users, use test data while you build.
</Callout>

<Challenge title="Write your brief, then prove the hardest part">
Write your project brief, then build a working prototype that proves the hardest part of your idea works. Don't build the easy stuff yet.

Build the risky part first: the AI feature, the tricky algorithm, the thing you're not sure is possible. Skip styling and polish.

Use any tools from the course: Lovable, Claude Code, Claude Design. Building needs access; see [how hands-on access works](/access). Then carry on to [Module 10](/module-10) to ship it and show it.
</Challenge>
