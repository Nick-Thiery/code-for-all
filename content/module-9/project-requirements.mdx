Before you build, check your idea against the six requirements.

<RequirementsDiagram />

1. **A clear goal and deliverables.** You can say what it does, and what "done" looks like.
2. **Legal and reasonable.** Nothing that breaks a rule, and nothing that would need a big company to run.
3. **Needs fewer than 15 employees.** In plain words: a small team could run it. You and a few friends.
4. **Real data behind any claims.** If it predicts something, publish the code. No fake statistics.
5. **Something you're interested in.** You'll be working on it for a while.
6. **Real value, not just an information site.** It should do something for someone, not only tell them things.

<CheckYourself>
  <Question q="A site that predicts tomorrow's bus delays, with the prediction code published and real timetable data behind it. Does it meet the requirements?">
    Yes, if it's something you're interested in and has a clear goal. It uses real data and publishes its code, it gives real value (a prediction people can act on), and a small team could run it.
  </Question>
  <Question q="A page that lists facts about your favourite football club. Does it meet the requirements?">
    Not on its own. That's an information site: it tells people things but doesn't do anything for them. Add something that gives real value, like a match-day planner or a quiz that saves scores.
  </Question>
  <Question q="An app that claims '90% of students who use it get better grades', with no data behind the number. Does it meet the requirements?">
    No. Any claim needs real data behind it, and no fake statistics. Drop the claim, or collect real data and show it.
  </Question>
</CheckYourself>
