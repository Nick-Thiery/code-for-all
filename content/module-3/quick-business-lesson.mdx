## What Snapiq does

[Snapiq](https://snapiq.tools) is a <Term>Chrome extension</Term>. It takes screenshots of lecture slides and web pages, and it can merge several screenshots into one image.

Why merge them? Many AI tutors limit how many images you can upload at once. One merged image gets round that limit, so you can ask about a whole lecture in one go.

It's on the Chrome Web Store. There's a free weekly allowance and a paid Pro plan. As of September 2026 it was rated 4.8 stars, from more than 13 reviews. You don't need Snapiq for this course, and nothing in this course asks you to pay for anything.

## The business lesson

**It's 30% the idea, 70% of the execution.**

Execution is the doing part: actually building your idea, and doing it well.

First-time <Term def="people who start a business.">founders</Term> will be searching for the perfect idea, product, service. They will keep these "good" ideas to themselves.

On the other hand, second-time founders will be shouting their ideas to everyone in hope that someone will care enough to consider it.

In plain words: people starting their first business hide their idea. People on their second business tell everyone, because they've learned that telling people helps.

People will rarely "steal" your idea. Execution is what actually matters.

Most ideas are good because they already exist.

So you don't need a brand-new idea for your Chrome extension. Screenshot tools already exist, and Snapiq is one of them. What counts is how well you build yours.

### An everyday comparison

Think of pancakes. Almost everyone knows the recipe: flour, eggs, milk. The idea isn't secret, and it isn't new. But some people's pancakes are much better than others. The difference is the doing: the pan, the timing, the practice, and listening when people say "a bit thinner next time". That's execution.

### A worked example

Here's a made-up example. Two friends, Sam and Priya, both want a Chrome extension that tidies their messy tabs.

- **Sam** keeps the idea secret. Sam spends weeks planning the perfect version and never shows anyone. Nothing gets built.
- **Priya** tells her friends about it. She builds a simple version in one afternoon: one button that groups tabs by website. A friend tries it and says, "I want a button to ungroup them too." Priya adds it.

Tab organisers already exist, so neither idea was new. Priya's is better because she built it, showed it to people and improved it. That's the 70%.

### What this means for you

- **Say your idea out loud.** Tell a friend or someone at your session. Their questions will make it better.
- **Don't worry if it already exists.** That shows people want it. Make yours work well, or work the way you like.
- **Build a small version first.** One feature that works beats ten that don't. You can add more later.
