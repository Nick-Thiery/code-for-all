Lovable builds what you ask for. Whatever you leave out, it guesses. So the way you ask decides what you get.

Think of asking someone to bake you a birthday cake. "Make me a cake" could get you anything. "A chocolate cake for ten people, with blue icing and Happy Birthday on top" gets you the cake you pictured. A <Term>prompt</Term> works the same way.

Here's the same request three times: bad, better and strong.

<PromptLadder>

<LadderPrompt level="Bad" prompt="Create a website for my course" leavesOut={["Role", "Target audience", "Core pages", "Design style", "Output required", "Key features"]}>

It doesn't say what the course is, who it's for, which pages it needs or how it should look. Lovable has to guess all of it.

</LadderPrompt>

<LadderPrompt level="Better" prompt="Create a modern website for my coding course with a homepage and lessons." leavesOutLabel="Still missing" leavesOut={["Role", "Target audience", 'Design style ("modern" is vague)', "Output required", "Key features"]}>

Better: it says what kind of course it is and names two pages. But "modern" could mean almost anything, and there's still no audience.

</LadderPrompt>

</PromptLadder>

The strong prompt has seven parts, each with one job.

<Callout kind="tip">
The bad prompt leaves out the audience, the pages and the style. Check your own prompts for those three first.
</Callout>

## Before you write: four questions

Lovable's own [prompting guide](https://docs.lovable.dev/prompting/prompting-one) says to answer four questions before you start:

1. **What is it?** For example, a one-page website.
2. **Who is it for?** This becomes your target audience.
3. **Why will they use it?** This shapes your goal.
4. **What's the one thing they should do there?** This becomes a key feature, like a button to press.

If you can't answer one, the AI can't either. It will guess.

## More tips from Lovable's guide

- **Describe the look in words.** Words like "playful", "minimal" or "bold" tell Lovable the style. "Bold, bright colours" tells it more than "colourful".
- **Use real words.** Write the actual heading or sentence you want, not "some text here". Otherwise you get <Term def="made-up filler words that sit where the real text will go.">placeholder text</Term>.
- **Let it ask.** You can end a prompt with: "Ask me any questions you need in order to fully understand what I want." Lovable may reply with questions before it builds.

## A worked example

Here's how the four questions turn into a prompt for an About me website. This example is about a made-up teenager, so nothing here is about a real person.

1. **What is it?** A one-page About me website.
2. **Who is it for?** Other teenagers and my family.
3. **Why will they use it?** To see what I like and what I've made.
4. **What's the one thing they should do there?** Look at my projects.

Each answer goes into a part of the strong prompt. "A one-page About me website" goes in the **Goal**. "Other teenagers and my family" is the **Target audience**. "Look at my projects" becomes a **Core page**: a Projects section. Then add the **Role**, the **Design style**, the **Output required** and the **Key features**, just like the strong prompt above.
