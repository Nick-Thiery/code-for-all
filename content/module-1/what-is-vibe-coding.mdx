<KeyTerm term="Vibe coding">
Building software by telling an AI what you want, in plain words. You decide what it should do; the AI writes the code.
</KeyTerm>

The skill in vibe coding is asking well. That's what most of this module is about.

## Vibe coding in three steps

1. **Describe.** Convert your product requirements into clear, conversational <Term term="prompt" def="the message you type to tell an AI what you want.">prompts</Term>. You define the rules; AI defines the architecture.
2. **Build.** AI will interpret requirements to generate responsive code, manage standard databases, and layout elements instantly.
3. **Launch.** Review the prototype, dynamically tweak specific features, sync with modern production tools, and push to cloud hosting under 5 seconds.

In plain words: you say what you want, the AI builds it, and you check it and put it online.

## An everyday comparison

Vibe coding is a bit like ordering food at a restaurant. You don't go into the kitchen and cook. You tell the waiter what you want. The kitchen makes it. Then you taste it and say what to change: "less salt next time".

The clearer your order, the closer the food is to what you wanted. "Something nice" gets you a surprise. "A cheese pizza, no olives, cut into squares" gets you exactly that.

## A worked example

Here's what the three steps look like for a small website. You'll build one like it in [lesson 6](/module-1/build-about-me).

1. **Describe.** You type: "Make a one-page website about my drawings, for my friends and family. Use a light background and bold headings."
2. **Build.** The AI makes the page: the layout, the headings, the colours and the code behind them. You watch it appear.
3. **Launch.** You look at it and ask for changes, like "Make the headings bigger". When you're happy, you put it online so other people can see it.

You never wrote a line of code. You made every decision.

## What can you build?

Apps, Chrome extensions, websites, trading algorithms, and more.

For example, [Snapiq](https://snapiq.tools/) is a <Term def="a small add-on that gives the Chrome browser a new ability.">Chrome extension</Term> for taking screenshots. One of this course's tutors built it.

Here are the stats from Lovable's analytics page for a website built with Lovable. In the last 30 days it had about 19,300 visits and 26,000 page views.

The picture comes from an older version of Lovable, which labelled this number "Visitors". Lovable now calls it <Term def="one browsing session on a website. The same person coming back later counts as a new visit.">visits</Term>, because it counts sessions, not people. If you come back tomorrow, that's a second visit.

## Getting users

A product needs people to use it. Some ways to find them are SEO (search engine optimisation), Google Ads, social media marketing and word of mouth.

SEO means making your site easy for Google to find. Word of mouth means people telling their friends.

For example, one Google Ads campaign for the Snapiq website got about 16,200 clicks for $134.
