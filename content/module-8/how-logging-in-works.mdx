Now the first thing your site can't do: it forgets everyone. Fixing it is called authentication. Here's how it works on any website or app.

<KeyTerm term="Authentication">
What lets your site remember who is here: signing up, logging in and logging out.
</KeyTerm>

## Sign up

When someone signs up on a website or app, it creates a new row in the user table. This table includes your email and a scrambled version of your password.

## Log in

When you try to log in, it verifies if your email and password match what is registered in the user table. If they match, it will hand your browser a token.

## Log out

Logging out throws your token away. This means the site stops recognising you until you log in again.

## What a token is

<KeyTerm term="Token">
A token is a temporary sticker that says "this is James". Your browser shows it on every request, so the site doesn't ask for your password on every single click. It expires on its own.
</KeyTerm>

## Notice what all 3 actions are

Your site is sending a request to another computer and getting an answer back. Logging in is just an API call!

It's the door from [lesson 2](/module-8/what-an-api-is) again: you knock with a request, and it answers.
