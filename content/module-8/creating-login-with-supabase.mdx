Your turn! You've seen [how logging in works](/module-8/how-logging-in-works). Now add it to your own page.

Supabase is a service that gives your site a database and <Term>authentication</Term>, so it can remember users. Its free plan is all you need.

## 1. Sign up for Supabase

This part needs access; see [how hands-on access works](/access). You sign in to Supabase with a GitHub account, so do this step in a session, with your teacher's go-ahead.

### Sign in

1. Go to [supabase.com](https://supabase.com) → click **Start your project** → **Continue with GitHub**.

   If GitHub asks whether Supabase can use your account, say yes (it may say **Authorize**). You should now see your Supabase dashboard.

### Make an organisation (first time only)

The first time, Supabase asks you to make an <Term def="a folder for your Supabase projects. You make one, the first time you use Supabase.">organisation</Term> before your first project.

2. **Name it.** Anything works, like `my-projects`. Leave out your full name and your school.
3. **Type.** If it asks what kind of organisation it is, pick the closest match, like a personal one. Not sure? Ask in your session.
4. **Plan: choose Free.** If anything asks for a card, stop: you're on the wrong plan.
5. **Click Create organization.**

### Make a project

You should now see a page called "Create a new project".

6. **Organization:** the one you just made.
7. **Project name:** anything, like `my-login-test`. No personal details.
8. Set up a database password and save it (this is **NOT** your login password).

   Type one, or use the option under the box that makes one for you, then copy it into your notes. This course never uses it, but keep it safe.
9. Pick the recommended region.

   You may see **Auto**, marked **Recommended**: keep it. If you're asked to choose, pick the one nearest you. For Singapore, that's **Southeast Asia (Singapore)** ([Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)).
10. **Leave the Security options as they are.** Keep **Enable Data API** ticked: the code Claude writes needs it.
11. **Click Create new project.** Setting it up takes a minute or two.

    You should now see your project's home page, with its name at the top.

## 2. Get your two values

In your Supabase project, there are three parts: the Publishable key (steps 1 to 3), the Project ID (steps 4 and 5) and turning off Confirm email (steps 6 to 8).

### The Publishable key

1. Go to **Project Settings**.

   It's in the sidebar on the left, with a gear icon.
2. Go to **API Keys**.

   It's under **Configuration**. You should now see a tab called **Publishable and secret API keys**. Stay on it.
3. Copy **Publishable key**.

   It's in the **Publishable key** section. Paste it into your notes. It's short, and starts with `sb_publishable_`. It's not a password: it tells Supabase **which project** is asking. Logging in is what tells it **who** is asking.

<Callout kind="headsup">
**Only copy the Publishable key.** On the same page there's a **Secret keys** section, and a tab for **Legacy anon, service_role API keys**. Don't copy anything from those.

The publishable key is made to go in a web page: Supabase says it's "safe to expose online", even in code on GitHub. A secret key or service_role key is the opposite. It gets past every safety rule in your project, so it must never go in your page, in a prompt for your page, or on GitHub ([Supabase API keys](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/api/api-keys)).
</Callout>

<Screenshot caption="Project Settings → API Keys: copy the key in the Publishable key section, not a secret key.">Supabase's API Keys page with the Publishable key section highlighted, the key itself blurred, from a neutral account</Screenshot>

### The Project ID

4. Go to **General**.

   It's in **Project Settings** too.
5. Copy **Project ID**.

   Paste it into your notes. It's a string of lowercase letters, like `xvljpkujuwroxcuvossw`. Your project's address is `https://` + your Project ID + `.supabase.co`.

**Shortcut:** the **Connect** button at the top of your project page also shows your Project URL and publishable key, ready to copy.

### Turn off Confirm email

6. Go to **Authentication**.

   It's in the sidebar on the left. Then, under **Configuration**, click **Sign In / Providers**.
7. Disable **Confirm email**.

   It's near the top, in the **User Signups** section. (On some dashboards it's inside the **Email** provider instead.) Switch it off.
8. Click **Save changes**.

   You should now see a message saying the settings were updated, and **Confirm email** stays off.

With **Confirm email** off, signing up logs you straight in. With it on, Supabase wants to send an email first ([Supabase sign-up reference](https://supabase.com/docs/reference/javascript/auth-signup)).

<Screenshot caption="Authentication → Sign In / Providers: switch off Confirm email, then click Save changes.">Supabase's Sign In / Providers page with the Confirm email switch turned off, from a neutral account</Screenshot>

Keep the Publishable key and the Project ID somewhere handy. They go into the prompt in the next step.

<Callout kind="headsup">
Turning off **Confirm email** is fine for practice, but don't do it for a real app. And when you try out your sign-up, use a test email, not your own.

**Why it has to be off for practice:** Supabase's built-in email only sends to people on your project's team, and only 2 emails an hour. So with **Confirm email** on, signing up with a test email fails ([Supabase email](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-smtp), [rate limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits)).

</Callout>

**Which test email?** One that's only for trying things out, like one your session gives you. While **Confirm email** is off, Supabase doesn't send it anything. Addresses like `test@example.com`, or anything at `example.com` or `test.com`, may be refused. If one is, use another.

## 3. Paste this into Claude

"This page" in the prompt means the page of the project you used in [Task 1](/module-8/put-live-data-on-your-page). Open that project in Claude Code first, the same way you did there.

```prompt title="Paste this into Claude"
Add email signup, login and logout to this page using Supabase.

Load supabase-js from
https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@supabase/supabase-js@2

Project URL: https://<your project id>.supabase.co
Publishable key: <paste yours>

When someone is logged in, show their email and a log out button.
```

Before you send it, swap `<your project id>` for the Project ID you copied in step 5, and `<paste yours>` for the Publishable key from step 3.

For example, if your Project ID were `xvljpkujuwroxcuvossw` (a made-up one), the Project URL line would end in `xvljpkujuwroxcuvossw.supabase.co`, with no `<` or `>` left.

Check that the key starts with `sb_publishable_`. If it starts with `eyJ` and is very long, you've copied an old-style key. Go back to step 2.

You can also add these two lines at the end of the prompt. They head off two common slips:

```prompt title="Example: two extra lines"
Call the Supabase client "client", not "supabase".
Only use the publishable key.
```

This will add:

1. One **script tag** that loads Supabase.
2. One line that connects using your two values.
3. Three buttons: sign up, log in, log out.

Your code will look different, but those first two parts look something like this ([supabase-js docs](https://supabase.com/docs/reference/javascript/installing)):

```html title="Example: the script tag and the line that connects"
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@supabase/supabase-js@2"></script>
<script>
  const client = supabase.createClient("https://YOUR-PROJECT-ID.supabase.co", "sb_publishable_PASTE_YOURS");
</script>
```

The three buttons each send one request to Supabase: `client.auth.signUp`, `client.auth.signInWithPassword` and `client.auth.signOut`. They're the three API calls from [lesson 4](/module-8/how-logging-in-works).

Then:

1. **Approve the changes** (**Yes** in the terminal, **Accept** in the desktop app).
2. **Push them**, as in [Task 1](/module-8/put-live-data-on-your-page): ask Claude Code to commit the changes and push them to GitHub. On a branch? Merge it with a pull request.
3. **Wait about a minute** for Vercel to update your live site.

Got questions? In a Code for All session, ask your tutor.

## 4. Check it worked

1. **Open your live site** and refresh it.

   You should now see an email box, a password box, and **Sign up** and **Log in** buttons. (Claude may label them a little differently.)
2. **Sign up.** Type your test email and a password of at least 6 characters, then click sign up.

   You should now see your test email on the page, and a log out button. If you see an error instead, find it in the Stuck box below.
3. **Refresh the page.** You should still be logged in: your browser kept your token, as in [lesson 4](/module-8/how-logging-in-works).

   If the sign-up form comes back instead, send Claude this example prompt: "When the page loads, check if someone is already logged in and show their email."
4. **Log out.** The email boxes come back.
5. **Log in** with the same test email and password. Your email shows again.
6. **Check Supabase.** In your project, click **Authentication**, then **Users**. Your test email is in the list: that's the new row in the user table.

**Check it worked:** on your live site you can sign up, log out and log in again, and your test user appears under **Authentication** → **Users**.

<Callout kind="tryit">
**See your token.** On your live site, while you're logged in, press F12 to open DevTools. In Chrome, click **Application**, then **Local storage**, then your site's address. Look for a name that starts with `sb-` and ends with `-auth-token`: that's your sticker and voucher from [lesson 4](/module-8/how-logging-in-works). Log out, and it disappears.
</Callout>

## Keep your project awake

Supabase pauses a free project after a week without use, and emails a warning first. Nothing is lost. To wake it up, open your Supabase dashboard, pick the project and click **Resume project**. You can do this for up to a year ([Supabase project pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)).
