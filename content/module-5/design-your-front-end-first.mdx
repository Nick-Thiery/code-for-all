In this module you'll design a website in Claude Design, hand it to Claude Code to build, then launch it. You'll see the whole workflow in [lesson 7](/module-5/the-whole-workflow). It starts with the design.

Claude Design runs in your browser, through your Code for All access. Nothing to install, nothing to pay.

## What Claude Design does

- Claude Design allows you to build the visual shell of your site.
- Describe how you want it to look without any code!

The <Term def="the part of a website people see and use: the words, the layout, the colours and the buttons.">front end</Term> is the part people see. A visual shell is the look of the site before it does anything. Making it actually work comes later, when you hand your design to Claude Code.

You chat with Claude about what you want, and it draws a working design on a <Term def="the big area beside the chat in Claude Design where your design appears. You can click on it, comment on it and move things around.">canvas</Term> beside the chat. ([Get started with Claude Design](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design))

### Think of it like building a house

Before anyone builds a house, an architect draws what it will look like. Everyone can see the drawing, point at the bits they want changed, and try ideas. Changing a drawing is quick. Moving a wall after it's built isn't.

Claude Design is the drawing. Claude Code, later in this module, is the builder.

## Start your design

<Callout kind="tryit">
Describe how you want your website to look, in plain words. No code needed.
</Callout>

These steps come from Anthropic's guide, [Get started with Claude Design](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design). Claude Design is new and still changing, so a button might look a little different. If it does, look for one that does the same job.

1. Open [Claude](https://claude.ai) in your browser, or the Claude desktop app, and log in with the access your Code for All session gives you.

2. Open Claude Design. There are three ways in. Use whichever you can see:

   - **From a chat:** start a new chat. In the message box, click **Output**, then choose **Design**.
   - **From the Artifacts tab:** click **Artifacts** in the sidebar, then pick a **Design** template.
   - **On its own site:** go to [claude.ai/design](https://claude.ai/design). It still works.

   If Claude Design asks you to pick a design system, you can skip it. You don't need one.

3. **Optional: add a picture of a site you like.** A screenshot helps Claude see the style you mean. Only use pictures with no names, faces or email addresses in them.

4. Describe your website. A good description says four things:

   - **The goal:** what the site is for.
   - **The layout:** what goes where on the page.
   - **The content:** the actual words, like the name and the headings.
   - **The audience:** who it's for.

   Here's an example. It's only an example: change it to your own idea.

   ```prompt title="Example: describe your design"
   Design a one-page website for my school's chess club, called Knight Moves.
   Goal: get new members to sign up.
   Layout: a big heading at the top, three short sections about what we do, and a sign-up form at the bottom with a box for a name and a Sign up button.
   Content: we meet on Tuesdays after school, and beginners are welcome.
   Audience: students aged 11 to 16.
   Style: bold and friendly, in dark green and white.
   ```

   Include a sign-up form, like the one in the example. You'll make it work in [lesson 4](/module-5/after-hand-off).

5. Send it. Claude may ask you some questions back before it starts. Answer them in the chat.

   You should now see your design appear on the canvas, beside the chat.

6. **Check it worked.** Look at the canvas. Can you see your site's name, your headings and the sign-up form? If something's missing, ask for it in the chat. Then leave the design open: in [the next lesson](/module-5/export-your-design) you'll export it.

## Improve your design

You don't have to get it right first time. There are four ways to change a design. ([Get started with Claude Design](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design))

- **Chat**, for big changes that affect the whole design. Type what you want, like this official example:

  ```prompt title="Example: try other layouts"
  Show me 2–3 alternative layouts for this page.
  ```

- **Inline comments**, for one part. Click directly on a part of the canvas, like a button, and type the change you want for just that part.
- **Edit directly.** Drag, resize and line things up on the canvas yourself.
- **Sliders.** Claude can add sliders for things like spacing and colour, so you can try settings without typing. ([Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design))

Ask for one change at a time, so you can see what each one did. Here are two more examples of changes you might ask for:

```prompt title="Example: one small change"
Make the Sign up button bigger and change it to orange.
```

```prompt title="Example: fix the wording"
Change the main heading to "Learn chess with us" and keep everything else the same.
```

### Before a big change, save what you have

Claude Design doesn't have version history yet, so there's no undo button for a whole design. Before you try something very different, tell Claude:

```prompt title="Save it first"
Save what we have and try a completely different approach.
```

### Find it again

Your design is private to you. Nobody else can see it unless you click **Share**, and you don't need to share it for this module. To open it again later, click the **Artifacts** tab in Claude's sidebar: it lists the things you've made. ([What are artifacts?](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17153992-what-are-artifacts-and-how-do-i-use-them))
