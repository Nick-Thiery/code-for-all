The original class ended with everyone pitching their project to the room. Here, you'll record a demo and show it to someone.

<DemoPlanDiagram />

## Record a 5-minute demo

Use any screen recorder: most laptops have one built in. Here's a plan for the five minutes.

1. **The problem.** Who has it, and why it matters. About one minute.
2. **Show it working.** Use it the way a real person would, from start to finish. About two minutes.
3. **What was hard.** The part you weren't sure you could build, and how you got it working. Show a prompt that failed and what you changed. About one minute.
4. **What's next.** One thing you'd add. About a minute.

<Callout kind="headsup">
Keep yourself out of the recording: screen only, no camera, no full name, no school. If your project shows any real data, use test data in the demo.
</Callout>

## Show it to someone

Show the demo to a friend, a parent, a teacher, or your Code for All session. Then ask them:

- What do you think this is for?
- Which part confused you?
- What would you want it to do next?

Write down their answers. If their answer to the first question doesn't match what you meant, that's the most useful thing you'll learn today.
