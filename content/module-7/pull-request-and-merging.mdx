## Open, review, merge

This part needs access; see [how hands-on access works](/access).

1. **Open PR.** After your push, a yellow **Compare & pull request** banner appears on the repo page. Click it, write one line about your change, then click **Create pull request**.
2. **Teammate reviews.** A teammate opens the **Files changed** tab: green lines were added, red lines removed. They comment, suggest edits, or say it looks good.
3. **Merge it.** Your teammate clicks **Merge pull request** → **Confirm merge**. Your branch joins main, and Vercel redeploys the live site automatically.

<Callout kind="headsup">
It's that easy. But remember, step two can only be done by you, not Claude. However, you can ask Claude to help you review the changes that your teammates made.

Step two here means this whole lesson: opening, reviewing and merging the pull request. It's not just item 2 in the list above.
</Callout>

The rest of this lesson walks through each step.

## Step by step: open a pull request

These steps come from GitHub's guide to [creating a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/create-pull-requests/creating-a-pull-request).

1. **Open your repo** on [github.com](https://github.com).
2. **Choose your branch.** Click the **branch dropdown** above the files and pick your branch, like alex-keyboard.
3. **Click Compare & pull request** in the yellow banner.

   No banner? Click the **Pull requests** tab at the top of the repo, then **New pull request**. Set **base** to main and **compare** to your branch, then click **Create pull request** ([GitHub Skills](https://github.com/skills/resolve-merge-conflicts)).

   You should now see a form with a box for a title and a bigger box for a description.
4. **Check base and compare.** At the top there are two branch menus with an arrow between them. **base** is where the changes go: it should say **main**. **compare** is where they come from: it should be your branch.
5. **Write a title and description.** One line is enough for the title, like "Add the keyboard". In the description, say what you changed and how to try it.
6. **Click Create pull request.**

   You should now see your pull request's own page, with its title at the top and a number, like #1.

If your repo is connected to Vercel, wait a minute. Vercel builds a <Term def="a private test copy of your site, made from a branch or pull request. It doesn't change your live site.">preview</Term> of your branch and adds a comment to the pull request with a **Visit Preview** link ([Vercel environments](https://vercel.com/docs/deployments/environments)). Click it to try your changes before they go live.

On Claude Code on the web, there's another way to open one: click **Create PR** at the top of the diff view ([Claude Code on the web](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/web-quickstart)). You still review and merge it on GitHub.

## Step by step: review it

A review is a second pair of eyes before anything reaches main. These steps come from GitHub's guide to [reviewing changes](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/review-pull-requests/reviewing-proposed-changes-in-a-pull-request).

1. **Open the Files changed tab** on the pull request. Each changed file is listed: green lines were added, red lines removed.
2. **Comment on a line** if something needs a question or a fix. Point at the line and add a comment.
3. **Finish the review.** At the top of **Files changed**, open the review box, choose **Comment**, **Approve** or **Request changes**, and click **Submit review**.

What makes a good review? Kind and specific. For example: "The keyboard looks great. Could the Enter key be wider, so it's easier to tap on a phone?"

### Working on your own?

GitHub says: "Pull request authors cannot approve their own pull requests" ([approving a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/approving-a-pull-request-with-required-reviews)). So you won't be able to click **Approve** on your own. That's fine: you can still read **Files changed** carefully, try the preview, and merge it yourself.

Claude can be your second pair of eyes. In Claude Code, on your branch, ask:

```prompt title="Example: a review from Claude Code"
Review my changes and suggest improvements.
```

To have Claude explain a teammate's branch, ask:

```prompt title="Example: explain a teammate's branch"
Fetch the latest changes from GitHub. Then explain, in plain words, what changed on the branch called <their branch name> compared with main.
```

Claude suggests; you decide. Opening, reviewing and merging stay your job.

## Step by step: merge it

These steps come from GitHub's guide to [merging a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/merge-and-close-pull-requests/merging-a-pull-request).

1. **Open the pull request.** Click the **Pull requests** tab, then click your pull request.
2. **Click Merge pull request**, near the bottom of the page.

   The button has a small arrow next to it with three ways to merge: **Create a merge commit**, **Squash and merge** and **Rebase and merge**. Leave it on the one GitHub has picked for you.
3. **Click Confirm merge.**

   You should now see that the pull request is marked as merged. Your branch has joined main.
4. **Optional: tidy up.** GitHub offers a **Delete branch** button near the bottom of the merged pull request. Changed your mind? **Restore branch** brings it back.

If **Merge pull request** is deactivated and GitHub talks about conflicts, two branches changed the same line. [Lesson 5](/module-7/merge-conflicts) explains what that means, and the [Wordle task](/module-7/recreate-wordle) shows how to fix it.

## Check it worked

1. On the repo's main page, check the branch dropdown says **main**. Open a file you changed: your changes should be there.
2. If your repo is connected to Vercel, wait a minute or two, then open your live site and refresh. Your change should be live. Only merges into main change the live site ([Vercel environments](https://vercel.com/docs/deployments/environments)).
