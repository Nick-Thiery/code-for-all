Time to build a first draft of the site you picked in [Today's task](/module-2/todays-task): a personality quiz, a news website or an online store.

A sprint is a short burst of work. In the original class, this one was 20 minutes. Take as long as you need.

There are three steps: write your prompt, paste it into Lovable, then save your prompt. Lovable changes often, so a button might look a little different from what's described here. If it does, look for one that does the same job.

## Build your first draft

This part needs access; see [how hands-on access works](/access).

1. **Draft a comprehensive descriptive prompt defining your target web portal layout.** In plain words: write a detailed <Term>prompt</Term> that describes your site and how it's laid out. ## Step 1: Write your prompt

Open a notes app or a document on your device, and write your prompt there. That way it's already saved somewhere safe.

Use the seven parts of the strong prompt from Module 1, one after another:

- **Role:** what kind of expert Lovable should be. For example, "an expert web designer".
- **Goal:** what to build, and its name.
- **Target audience:** who will use it.
- **Core pages:** every page, and what goes on each one.
- **Design style:** how it should look, in real words.
- **Output required:** what you want back. For Lovable, a working website.
- **Key features:** the must-haves, like working on a phone.

A few tips from Lovable's own [prompting guide](https://docs.lovable.dev/prompting/prompting-one):

- **Describe the look with style words**, like "playful", "minimal" or "bold". "Bold, saturated colours" tells Lovable more than "colourful".
- **Ask for real words**, not placeholder text. A quiz needs real questions, a news site real headlines.
- **Say "No login or database yet."** Lovable's own starter prompt ends with that line: there's less to go wrong. ([Lovable's Getting started guide](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/getting-started))
- **Leave out personal details.** No full names, school, address, phone number or photos of you.

### Example prompts

Here's one example prompt for each site. They're examples only: use one as a model, but make your own with your own ideas. Copying one word for word won't teach you much.

```prompt title="Example: personality quiz"
Role: You are an expert web designer who makes fun quiz websites.
Goal: Create a personality quiz website called "Which noodle are you?". It has five questions with four answers each, and six possible results.
Target audience: Teenagers who want a quick, funny quiz to share with friends.
Core pages:
1. Start page (the quiz name, one line about it and a big Start button)
2. Question pages (one question at a time, four answer buttons and a progress bar)
3. Results page (which noodle you are, an emoji, a short funny description and a Try again button)
Design style:
1. Playful and bright
2. Cream background with one orange accent colour
3. Big rounded buttons and friendly headings
Output required: A working website I can open and edit.
Key features:
1. Works well on a phone
2. Every answer counts towards a result
3. Real questions and results, not placeholder text
4. No login or database yet
```

```prompt title="Example: news website"
Role: You are an expert designer of news websites.
Goal: Create a news website called "The Weekly Byte" with short articles about science and technology.
Target audience: Students aged 13 to 16 who are curious about science but don't like long articles.
Core pages:
1. Home page (the top story in a big card, then the six latest stories)
2. Story page (headline, date, a picture, the article and three related stories)
3. Topic pages for Space, Games, Gadgets and Nature
4. About page (what the site is for)
Design style:
1. Clean and bold, like a magazine
2. White background, black headings and one bright blue accent
3. Big headlines that are easy to scan
Output required: A working website I can open and edit.
Key features:
1. Works well on a phone
2. A menu to switch between topics
3. Six short example articles, each labelled "Example story"
4. No login or database yet
```

```prompt title="Example: online store"
Role: You are an expert designer of online shops.
Goal: Create an online store for a made-up stationery brand called "Inkwell" that sells notebooks, pens and stickers.
Target audience: Students who like nice stationery and want to find a gift.
Core pages:
1. Home page (a big banner, three best-selling products and a Shop now button)
2. Shop page (twelve products as cards with a picture, a name and a price, and filters for Notebooks, Pens and Stickers)
3. Product page (a bigger picture, a description, the price and an Add to basket button)
4. Basket page (the items, the total and a Checkout button)
Design style:
1. Calm and minimal
2. Soft green and white, with dark grey text
3. Rounded cards with lots of space
Output required: A working website I can open and edit.
Key features:
1. Works well on a phone
2. The basket adds up the total
3. Checkout only shows a thank-you message: no real payments
4. No login or database yet
```

The news example asks for "Example story" labels because Lovable will make the articles up. Made-up news shouldn't look real.

**Check your prompt:** does it say who the site is for, list every page and describe the look? If so, you're ready.

## Step 2: Paste it into Lovable

These steps come from Lovable's [Getting started guide](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/getting-started).

1. Copy your whole prompt: select it, then press Ctrl+C (Cmd+C on a Mac).
2. Open [Lovable](https://lovable.dev) and log in with the access your Code for All session gives you.

   You should now see the dashboard. In the middle is a big box for your prompt. It says "Ask Lovable to create…". If you see one of your projects instead, click the menu icon at the top left to get back to the dashboard.

3. Check the <Term def="the menu by Lovable's prompt box that picks Build, Chat or Plan. Build is the one that makes things.">mode picker</Term> beside the send button. It should say **Build**.

   If it says **Chat** or **Plan**, click it and choose **Build**. Chat only talks about your idea, and Plan writes a plan first. Lovable remembers the last mode you picked in that browser, so check it each time. ([Lovable's guide to the dashboard](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/dashboard-overview))

4. Click in the box and paste your prompt: Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac). Then click the send button.
5. Pick a look. Lovable may show you **three design directions** side by side, or ask a few questions about fonts, colours and layout. Click the one closest to your design style, then click **Submit**.

   Your prompt already describes the look, so Lovable may skip this and start building straight away. Both are fine. Picking a look doesn't cost anything extra. ([Lovable's guide to design directions](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/design-guidance))

   <Screenshot caption="Lovable's three design directions for a first prompt, side by side.">Lovable showing three design directions for a quiz website, with a Describe changes box and a Submit button, from a neutral account with no names or avatars</Screenshot>

6. Wait while it builds. The first version takes a few minutes, and you can watch it take shape.

   If Lovable stops to ask you a question, pick an answer or skip it.

7. You should now see the editor: the chat on the left and your new site, the <Term def="the live version of your website in Lovable's editor. It changes as Lovable builds.">preview</Term>, on the right.

   There's no save button. Lovable saves every change as a version for you. ([Lovable's guide to the chat](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/projects/chat))

8. Click around the preview like a visitor would. Then click **Mobile view** above the preview to see your site at phone size, and **Desktop view** to go back. ([Lovable's guide to the preview](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/projects/preview))
9. **Check it worked.** You should see your site in the preview, and it should be the kind of site you picked: a quiz, a news site or a shop.

Building uses Lovable <Term def="Lovable's allowance for building. Each change you ask for uses some.">credits</Term>. On Lovable's Free plan you get 5 a day, and a whole new site uses more than a small change. You'll need credits for one more full build in [lesson 4](/module-2/using-refined-prompts), so don't spend them all on small fixes today. ([Lovable's guide to credits](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/credits-and-usage))

## Step 3: Save your exact prompt

You'll need this exact prompt in the next lesson, so save it now.

1. If you wrote your prompt in a notes file, check it's the version you pasted. If you changed anything in Lovable's box before sending, change it in your notes too. Your prompt is also in Lovable's chat, as your first message, if you need to copy it again.
2. Paste it into the box below as well. It stays on this device, and the next two lessons show it back to you.

<SaveHere id="solo-sprint-prompt" label="Your exact prompt for Lovable" placeholder="Paste the prompt you gave Lovable, exactly as you typed it." />

3. Give your project a name you'll recognise, like "Noodle quiz, first draft". Go to the dashboard, click **⋯** on the project's card, then **Edit**, then **Name**. ([Lovable's guide to the dashboard](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/dashboard-overview))
4. **Check it worked.** Your prompt should be in your notes file and in the box above. Your project should be in **Recents** in the dashboard sidebar, with its new name.

## Look at your first draft

It's only a first draft. Look at what Lovable got right and what it had to guess. Did it make the pages you listed? Did it pick colours or words you didn't ask for?

Jot down two or three things you'd change. Don't fix them one by one yet. The next two lessons make your prompt better, so the next version guesses less.
