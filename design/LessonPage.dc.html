How a language model works

Find out how an AI tool like Claude turns your words into an answer, one small piece at a time.

When you send Claude a message, it doesn't look up an answer somewhere. It writes a brand-new reply, a little at a time. The part doing the writing is called the . Model: the trained AI system that generates the text.

It's a very good guesser

A language model learned by reading a huge amount of text: books, websites, code and more. From all that reading, it picked up patterns. Now, when it sees some words, it can guess which word probably comes next.

It picks a word, adds it to the sentence, then guesses again. It does this over and over, very fast, until the answer is done.

Words become tokens

Models don't read whole words the way you do. They split text into smaller pieces first.

A long word like “badminton” might be split into two or three tokens. Short, common words like “the” are usually just one.

Code is made of tokens too

When you ask an AI agent to build a website, it predicts code the same way it predicts words. Every piece below was a guess about what should come next.
