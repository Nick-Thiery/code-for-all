2All 10 states for 1a · light and dark

1 · Empty. Button is pale, dashed and unclickable, with a plain note beside it.

2 · Near the limit. Counter turns accent and bold, with a calm tip. At 1,200 the box stops accepting text.

3 · Loading. Three hexagons breathe in turn; announced as a status. Prompt stays visible, read-only.

4 · Weak result. Quote form of the fix.

5 · Middling result. Missing form: a dashed "+" slot instead of a quote, since there's nothing to point at.

6 · Strong, second try. "Since your last try" band: the new pip pops in with a sky-blue ripple, then three small hexes light up by the label. Fix becomes a stretch idea.

7 · Off-topic. Neutral hexagon, one nudge back to the task, button returns focus to the box.

8 · Something went wrong. Blame is placed on us in the first line; their prompt is reassured as safe.

9 · Hourly limit. Button disabled; no countdown, nothing that feels like a penalty.

10 · Daily limit. "For everyone" makes it clear it isn't about them.

Lesson 3 of 8

Telling AI what you want

An AI coding tool only knows what you tell it. If you say "make it nice", it has to guess what nice means to you, and it usually guesses wrong.

Good prompts do three things. They say exactly what you want, they explain your situation, and they ask for a sensible amount at once.

Have a go below. You can try as many times as you like.

Accessibility

Feedback is an <article> labelled by its headline; focus moves to the headline when a result or notice arrives. Reading order: headline → progress → scores → fix → rewrite → try again. Loading container is role="status" (polite). Skill names are disclosure buttons with aria-expanded; pips are aria-hidden, words carry the score. Progress line has a screen-reader sentence ("Context went from Getting there to Nailed it."). Counter and limit message are linked with aria-describedby; limit message is aria-live. maxlength 1200. Try again focuses the textarea with the caret at the end; text is never cleared.
